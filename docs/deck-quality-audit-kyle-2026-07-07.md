## Main Findings

1. **The corpus is heavily skewed toward product-pitch and onboarding narratives.** The system is already better at warm onboarding decks and product concepts than at rigorous decision decks, QBRs, data readouts, or McKinsey-style analyses.
2. **Accuracy risk is the largest systemic gap.** Many decks contain confident market, growth, or financial claims without explicit sources, assumptions, or provenance in notes. This is especially risky for investor, market, and strategy decks.
3. **Decks often sound polished before they are precise.** Titles and copy lean on broad phrases like "financial freedom", "seamless", "empowerment", and "reimagined". The highest-quality tech-company decks are usually more specific: decision, trade-off, metric, customer segment, or operating consequence.
4. **The system needs deck-type-specific structures.** A product roadmap should show sequencing and trade-offs; a UX research deck should show methodology, evidence, quotes, and recommendations; a consulting analysis should show issue tree, options, recommendation, sensitivity, and next steps.
5. **Slides need stronger evidence handling.** The right behavior is not "avoid numbers"; it is "separate fact, assumption, and recommendation" and put sources/assumptions in notes.

## Recommended Product Improvements

1. **Add deck-type classification before generation.** Detect the deck family from prompt + files, then inject the correct narrative arc, required evidence, preferred slide patterns, and anti-patterns.
2. **Create an accuracy/provenance gate.** For market, investor, QBR, data, and strategy decks, require every numeric or external factual claim to be labeled as source-backed, user-provided, or assumption.
3. **Use different quality rubrics by deck type.** A product pitch should be scored on problem intensity and differentiation; a roadmap on sequencing and trade-offs; a UX research deck on methodology and evidence; a McKinsey-style deck on issue tree logic and recommendation clarity.
4. **Add a pre-export deck audit.** Show a compact scorecard before PPTX export: relevance, evidence risk, specificity, density, narrative arc, and deck-type fit.
5. **Teach stronger title writing.** Generate action titles that state the takeaway, not topic labels. Example: "Premium Can Increase Retention If It Owns Repeat Transfers" is stronger than "Félix Premium".
